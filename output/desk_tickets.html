Reflections

How the team did on the final run (after a fresh reset), compared with my first try in Problem 9. The evidence is on the ticket and Cash tabs.

1. How I'd grade each agent, and why

I graded each agent on three things: did it get the facts right, did it follow the shop's rules, and did it only do the work the ticket actually needed. Campus Customs is a small student shop on Chapel Street. On the shop date it had $3,400 in checking and $3,240 in bills due that week, so a wrong number or a wasted step costs real money. The grades are for the final run, with the Problem 9 grade next to each one.

BossA−Problem 9: B

The Boss improved the most. It asked the right specialist first on all three tickets and only brought in the people it needed. On Tauhid's tee order (#101) it checked the bank balance itself and saw that $3,400 covered both the overdue $840 invoice and the $2,400 rent, so it asked for approval to pay the invoice on the first try. In Problem 9 it held that invoice back “because rent was due” and needed a second run. This time it also waited until it had all the facts before asking Customer Service to write the reply, so Customer Service didn't have to look anything up. On the AI Club's hoodies (#103) it gave the biggest discount the rules allow, 20%. That still left a 52.6% margin, and it required the $464 deposit.

What holds it back is tokens. About 42k of the 54k tokens on #101 were the Boss alone, because it re-reads its long instructions every time it takes a step. On one early attempt it also passed internal invoice details to Customer Service, and they ended up in a customer draft (more on that below).

AccountingAProblem 9: A−

Accounting did the most with the least. On the rent notice (#102) it checked the landlord's claim against the lease ($2,400, due September 2, two days out). It made sure we hadn't already paid or queued the rent, and told the Boss we'd have $160 left, which is a low-cash warning. On the hoodies it made one price lookup and one vendor lookup, and the numbers matched my prediction to the cent. Its two checks for an existing payment are technically extra, since the system already refuses double payments. But they're cheap and they protect the shop's biggest bill, so I'd keep them.

InventoryA−Problem 9: B

In Problem 9, Inventory made 8 lookups on #101's first run because it got asked twice, and 4 on the second. Now it makes 3. One stock lookup showed every size of the tee (S 0, M 5, L 3, XL 2), and it found the 5-day reprint time. Most importantly, it noticed that invoice 501 was a rush reprint of Tauhid's exact size, and that's what convinced the Boss to pay it. The one small waste is that it read the invoice even though the Boss sees the same invoice in its own cash lookup. It wasn't needed on the hoodie ticket at all, which is right.

Customer ServiceB+Problem 9: B

Customer Service now writes its drafts with no lookups and no handoffs, using about 3k tokens each. The replies are short, friendly, and only use real numbers: Tauhid's three sizes and the reprint date, and the club's $46.40 each, $928.00 total and $464.00 deposit. The problem is that it does what it's told even when that breaks its own rules. On the first attempt after my fixes, the Boss mentioned “the invoice,” and Customer Service told Tauhid the reprint would happen “after the invoice is processed.” Customers should never hear about the shop's bills.   • the ticket is about the Chapel Street space itself

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

The rent notice (#102) is the clearest case. Even in the lean run, the Boss just asks Accounting to look things up and then acts on the answer. One agent with the lease, payment and approval tools could make the same lookups with no handoff. That handoff cost 35k tokens on a ticket whose whole answer is “$2,400, due September 2, it matches the lease, pay it.”

The hoodie quote (#103) is close behind. The whole decision is one price lookup. The final run needed Accounting to make that lookup and Customer Service to turn it into four sentences. One agent with the price and vendor tools could do both.

Tauhid's tee (#101) is the one place the team earned its keep. Inventory connected invoice 501's description to Tauhid's size S, and that's what got the bill paid. Still, one agent could make the same connection with the same three lookups.

Why one agent would be simpler: the lookups are lean now (18 in total), but the three tickets still used about 128k tokens. Every handoff starts a new agent that reads its own long instructions, and the Boss re-reads its instructions and every report at each step. For a shop with three tickets, one agent that knew the shop's rules would be cheaper and easier to debug. Those rules are:

  • the discount tiers
  • the 40% margin floor
  • the 50% deposit
  • a person signing every payment
  • the $500 low-cash warning

What the team does give me is safety and a clear record. Only the Boss can ask for a payment or change a ticket, and no agent can approve a payment or record money coming in. The huddle on the board shows exactly who looked up what. But those protections come from the code and the MCP server, not from having five agents, so one agent could keep them too. The team setup would start paying off once the shop has more kinds of tickets than one set of instructions can handle well.
